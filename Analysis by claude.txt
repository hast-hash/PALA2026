# The Fall of Robespierre (1794): Stylometric Analysis Report

## Terminology Note (to avoid confusion)

Two different segmentation schemes are used in this study. To avoid confusion, the following terms are used consistently throughout:

- **Split Point 1/2/3**: The BEFORE/AFTER boundaries detected within Act I by Step 1 (Rolling Delta) at word positions 1000/1350/1850. Step 3's detailed verification was performed on these three points.
- **Segment 1, 2, 3, 4-1, 4-2**: Five contiguous sub-divisions of Act I (words 0–792 / 793–1091 / 1092–1579 / 1580–1902 / 1903–2068). Step 2(b) compared these five segments together with Act II and Act III.

The two schemes use different boundary logic (a single before/after split vs. multiple contiguous regions), so they partially overlap but do not correspond one-to-one.

---

## 1. Research Objective

This study examines whether Coleridge's style in Act I of *The Fall of Robespierre* (traditionally attributed solely to Coleridge) shows detectable stylistic change — potential influence from Southey — before versus after the collaborative planning meeting and division of labor with Southey (who wrote Acts II and III, with Act III originally drafted by Lovell and rewritten by Southey). Quantitative stylometric methods are used to test this hypothesis.

### 2.3 Documentary/Historical Background

- The collaborative planning session took place in August 1794, arising from a "playful conversation" at Robert Lovell's house.
- Southey and Lovell finished their assigned acts first; Coleridge continued writing Act I into the following evening (a record exists of 800 lines being written in two days). **This does not contradict the historical plausibility that the later portions of Act I were written after Coleridge had seen Southey's completed draft.**
- The song in the Adelaide scene was later published independently by Coleridge as "Domestic Peace," confirming his authorship of that passage beyond doubt.

## 3. Step 0.5: Bootstrap Consensus Tree (W=500, S=50, MFW=50–200 in steps of 25; 7 replicate trees)

- **CoSou_Act1, Act2, and Act3 clustered together before joining any external reference** (Act1–Act2: 100% support; +Act3: 86% support).
- Among external references, Southey (*Wat Tyler*, *Botany-Bay Eclogues*) and Baillie's *The Tryal* clustered at 71% support, while Coleridge (*Osorio*, *Triumph*) and Wordsworth's *Borderers* also clustered at 71% — **no clean two-author separation emerged**.
- Support values generally ranged 71–86%, never reaching the "highly robust" 90%+ threshold for external-reference groupings.

**Interpretation**: The finding that all three acts cluster together ahead of any external reference was an early, important warning that **shared subject matter/topic may dominate over authorial signal**.

## 4. Step 1 (Author-Attribution Phase 1): Whole-Play Rolling Delta (W=500, S=50, MFW=100, 9 references)

CSV analysis of the nearest reference at each word position revealed:

| Word Range | Actual Nearest Reference |
|---|---|
| ~250–900 | Coleridge_Osorio |
| **900–2000** (largest span within Act I) | **Baillie_Basil** |
| Most of 2450–5450 (Acts II–III) | **Baillie_Basil / Baillie_De Monfort alternating** |
| Southey nearest only at | 2100–2200, 4500–5100, 5500–5987 |

**Even within the known-Southey acts (II and III), the most frequent nearest reference was not Southey's own works but Baillie's.** Baillie's two works (~39,477 words combined) are far larger than Southey's reference set (9,805 words combined). Because Step 1's windowing method builds separate μ/σ centroids for each reference from its own internal sliding windows, **longer references yield more windows and thus more stable (lower-noise) centroid estimates — giving them a structural advantage in "winning" the nearest-neighbor comparison** regardless of genuine stylistic similarity. Results from the full 9-reference Step 1 run should not be used as direct evidence for authorship, due to this corpus-size artifact.

## 5. Step 1 (Author-Attribution Phase 2): Act I Rolling Delta (W=300, S=25, MFW=100)

### 5.1 Three Detected Split Points

| Split Point | Position | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Split Point 1 | word 1000 (range 850–1150) | Couthon's soliloquy revealing his true feelings → Robespierre/Barrere's political scheming |

| Split Point 3 | word 1850 (range 1700–2000) | Romantic/private scene (Adelaide) |

### 5.2 The Baillie Contamination Issue and Its Resolution

With six references active (including Baillie_Basil), **the actual nearest reference at all three split points was Baillie_Basil** (0.827, 0.817, and 0.847 respectively). The chart's "Southey 1 / Coleridge / Southey 2" labels reflected crossings among only the four Coleridge/Southey lines, not the true nearest neighbor across all six lines.

**After excluding Baillie_Basil**, the nearest reference reverted to plausible alternation between Coleridge and Southey:

| Split Point | 1st | 2nd | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Position 1000 | Wat Tyler 0.826 (Southey) | Botany-Bay 0.863 (Southey) | Clear gap |
| Position 1325 | Wat Tyler 0.924 (Southey) | Osorio 0.936 (Coleridge) | Narrow (0.012); actual crossing near word 1350 |
| Position 1850 | Wat Tyler 0.921 (Southey) | Osorio 0.923 (Coleridge) | Essentially tied (0.002) |

**Conclusion**: The Coleridge/Southey crossings underlying split points 1, 2, and 3 are not entirely an artifact and are substantively real, but the crossings at positions 1325 and 1850 are extremely narrow and should be interpreted with caution.

## 6. Step 2(a): Act-Level Classic Delta + Dendrogram + PCA

MFW=100 (window/step size settings are irrelevant to Step 2, which builds a single frequency vector from each document's full token set).

| | 9-reference set: Nearest | 4-reference set (Baillie et al. excluded): Nearest |
|---|---|---|
| Act 1 | Act3 (1.060) < Osorio (1.080) | Act2 (1.164) < Osorio (1.182) |
| Act 2 | Act3 (1.042) < Osorio (1.171) | Act1 (1.164) < Triumph (1.244) |
| Act 3 | Act2 (1.042) < Botany-Bay (1.245) | Act2 (1.175) < Triumph (1.421) |

**For all three acts, the nearest neighbor was always another act — never an external reference.** This pattern persists even after removing Baillie's large works, indicating it is **not** a corpus-size artifact but a more fundamental phenomenon: **the three acts share subject matter, characters, and vocabulary by virtue of being parts of the same play.** Note that "Acts II and III merge first" holds only in the 9-reference version; in the 4-reference version, Act1–Act2 is marginally the smallest pairwise distance (all three act-pairs lie within 0.011–0.012 of one another — a razor-thin margin).

**Implication**: The dendrogram's clustering topology in Step 2(a) should not be treated as direct evidence of authorship. Instead, the ranked distance from each act to each individual reference should be examined.

## 8. Step 3: Detailed Split-Point Comparison (dz-ref = 4 texts; MFW=100 baseline, 30/50/100/200/261 sensitivity sweep)

### 8.1 Split Point 1 (position 1000)

**PHASE 1**: Delta(Before, After) = 1.3650; minimum Before-distance = 1.2966 (Osorio); minimum After-distance = 1.3186 (Triumph). The nearest reference remained Coleridge throughout.

**Reference ranking**: **Both** Wat Tyler and Botany-Bay move closer (+0.1185, +0.0616). Triumph moves only marginally closer (+0.0065). Osorio moves farther away (−0.0855).

**General Imposters (GI) test**: With windowImposters ON (1,170 pooled imposters), the symmetric score was 0.028 — strongly against common authorship. The OFF configuration (imposterRatio=1.0) was found to be invalid, since it eliminates randomness on the imposter side.

**PHASE 2 (MFW sensitivity)**: **At MFW=30–50, all four references diverge uniformly** (both Southey and Coleridge move farther away, with no distinction). Only from MFW=100 upward does a Southey-leaning convergence appear.

**PHASE 3–5 (MFW=30 re-run)**: Political vocabulary essentially flat (+0.00188); modest increases in emotional vocabulary and archaic second-person pronouns. The rising frequency of "Barrere" is explicable by the shift from an ensemble scene to a two-hander dialogue. Average word length and sentence length are essentially unchanged.

**Summary**: Of the three split points, this shows the broadest pattern — simultaneous approach toward both Southey reference works — but the GI test's methodological caveat and the low-MFW divergence weaken this interpretation.

### 8.2 Split Point 2 (position 1350)

**PHASE 1**: Delta = 1.4100; minimum Before-distance = 1.2574 (Triumph); minimum After-distance = 1.3779 (Botany-Bay). **The nearest reference shifted from Coleridge to Southey.**

**Reference ranking**: **Only** Botany-Bay moves closer (+0.0258). Wat Tyler, Triumph, and Osorio all move farther away (roughly −0.13 each).

**GI test**: Symmetric score 0.040 — strongly against common authorship.

**PHASE 2**: **Only Botany-Bay shows a consistent "closer" trend across all MFW values (30–261)** (as large as −0.327 at MFW=30) — not a general Southey effect, but specific to *Botany-Bay Eclogues*.

**PHASE 3**: "Couthon" disappears (−0.00444); "Adelaide" appears (+0.00343) — a clear trace of the character-cast change.

**PHASE 4**: Political vocabulary −0.01179; emotional vocabulary +0.01083; archaic second-person +0.01184.

**PHASE 5**: Average word length and sentence length essentially unchanged.

**Summary**: The apparent "approach toward Southey" is, in substance, a selective approach to a single work (*Botany-Bay Eclogues*), plausibly explained by shared thematic content (loss of home/family). The concurrent character-cast change indicates this is very likely a scene-transition artifact rather than a genuine authorial signal.

### 8.3 Split Point 3 (position 1850)

**PHASE 1**: Delta = 1.3505; minimum Before-distance = 1.0920 (Osorio); minimum After-distance = 1.6155 (Wat Tyler — though in absolute terms this is far worse than the Before minimum).

**Reference ranking**: **All four references move substantially farther away** (Osorio −0.7267, Triumph −0.5302, Botany-Bay −0.4093, Wat Tyler −0.3794).

**GI test**: Symmetric score 0.075 (asymmetric: After-vs-Before = 0.030, but Before-vs-After = 0.120 — a notable directional discrepancy).

**PHASE 2**: All four references diverge consistently across every MFW value.

**PHASE 3**: Words such as "tyrant," "convention," "assassin," and "fate" increase — the opposite direction from Split Point 2. This is likely because the After window (only 374 tokens) spans both the tail of the Adelaide scene and the return to political urgency (entrance of Billaud Varennes), mixing two registers.

**PHASE 4**: Political vocabulary slightly increases (+0.00244); emotional vocabulary +0.00566; archaic second-person nearly flat (+0.00088) — a smaller, more mixed pattern than Split Point 2.

**Summary**: Not an approach toward Southey, but rather isolation of the After segment from every author considered. This aligns with the isolation of Segment 4-2 found in Step 2(b), and most plausibly reflects instability from a very short fragment. **This split point should not be treated as usable evidence.**

## 9. Cross-Comparison: Disentangling Genre Effects from Genuine Stylistic Influence

| Split/Segment | Key Indicators | Genre Effect or Stylistic Influence? |
|---|---|---|
| Split Point 1 (pos. 1000) | Both Southey works move closer; GI negative | Broad lean toward Southey generally, not one text — harder to explain away by coincidence of a single theme |
| Split Point 2 (pos. 1350) | Only Botany-Bay converges across all MFW; character-cast change (Couthon→Adelaide) | Product of a scene transition; thematic coincidence specific to *Botany-Bay Eclogues* (loss/domesticity) |
| Split Point 3 (pos. 1850) | All four references diverge (isolation) | Not a genre effect but statistical instability from a very short fragment (374 tokens); essentially undecidable |
| Segment 4-1 (private scene) | Best link to Act III, but assessment varies by visualization method | Dominated by the romantic/private-scene theme; possibly also reflects a genuine gap — no comparably private/domestic Coleridge reference text exists in the corpus |
| Segment 4-2 (political-return scene) | Uniformly isolated from all references (robust across visualization methods) | Statistical instability from being too short; not usable as evidence |
| Whole Act I (Step 2a) | Acts I, II, and III are consistently closer to each other than to any external reference | A shared-topic effect across all three acts; individual split points' "approach toward Southey" may partly be reflecting this broader, play-wide topical proximity rather than anything specific to that split point |

**On "the thematic effect of Botany-Bay"**: Southey's *Botany-Bay Eclogues* (written 1794–98) are pastoral poems centered on convicts transported to the Australian penal colony, repeatedly dealing with the loss of home and family, private grief, and solitary confession. These themes closely parallel the Adelaide scene (a private monologue on the memory of a father, domestic peace, and lost happiness). Rather than reflecting an approach toward Southey's authorial fingerprint, this proximity can be explained by a theme-independent phenomenon: **similar vocabulary (emotional words, intimate second-person address, domestic terms) tends to increase whenever a similar subject is treated, regardless of who is writing.**

## 10. Overall Conclusion

### Converging Evidence

- Split Point 1 (position 1000) shows a broader pattern than any other split point — simultaneous convergence toward both Southey reference works.
- Split Point 2 and Segment 4-1 both show convergence mediated by a specific theme (loss/domesticity in Botany-Bay Eclogues; Adelaide's private monologue), consistent with an explanation rooted in topic rather than authorship.

### Contradictions and Points Requiring Caution

- Split Point 3 and Segment 4-2 cannot serve as usable evidence either way — they are neutral with respect to the hypothesis.
- The "approach toward both Southey works" at Split Point 1 has not yet been fully disentangled from the broader whole-act topical proximity confirmed in Step 2(a) (i.e., the entire Act I already shows some proximity to Act III regardless of authorship). A further test — comparing Segment 1 (word 0–792, unambiguously within Coleridge's own established range) as a baseline distance to Act III — would help settle this.

### Overall Judgment

**Split Point 1 (position 1000) alone provides the relatively strongest evidence supporting the hypothesis of stylistic change following the collaborative meeting with Southey.** However, the underlying claim that "this portion was written after the meeting" remains a working hypothesis under test throughout this study; the Step 3 results provide evidence **not inconsistent with** that hypothesis, rather than direct proof of it. Rather than stating this "has been confirmed," the most accurate summary is: **the existence of stylistic change has been detected by multiple indicators, but whether its cause is Southey's influence versus a coincidence of theme or narrative structure remains not fully resolved by the current data.**

The apparent "approach toward Southey" at Split Point 2 and Segment 4-1, upon closer examination, resolves into selective convergence toward a single work (*Botany-Bay Eclogues*), plausibly explained by a coincidence of theme (loss/domesticity) or by a gap in the reference corpus (no comparable private-scene Coleridge text available) — both of which are more naturally explained by factors other than authorship. Split Point 3 and Segment 4-2 most likely reflect statistical instability from very short fragments and should not be used as evidentiary material.

## 11. Outstanding Issue (Outside the Scope of This Analysis)

- **It has been suggested that Acts II and III themselves may contain later additions by Coleridge.** If true, this would undermine the premise — used throughout this analysis — of treating Acts II and III as a "pure Southey baseline." This question is left to future bibliographic verification (comparison of textual variants, correspondence, etc.).

## 12. Methodological Notes for Future Replication

- Step 1 and the GI method's windowImposters option can produce a structural advantage for longer reference texts, since more internal windows yield more stable centroid estimates (confirmed with Baillie's two works being roughly four times the size of Southey's reference set). Any analysis using large references should always be cross-checked against a version with those references excluded.
- The phenomenon confirmed in Step 2(a) — acts consistently closer to each other than to any external reference — is distinct from a corpus-size artifact; it persists even after excluding Baillie's large works, indicating a genuine shared-topic effect.
- PCA scatter plots and dendrograms built from the same distance matrix can yield different visual impressions (confirmed with Segment 4-1). Always cross-check both, and treat the raw distance matrix as the final authority.
- The GI method is effectively invalid when imposterRatio=1.0 and windowImposters is OFF. windowImposters should be ON by default; the window/step size used for this imposter-pool expansion should not simply be inherited from Step 1's fine-grained settings (300/25) but adjusted to something coarser (e.g., 600/400) to reduce redundancy — this adjustment was recommended but not implemented in the present study.
- For fragments under 300 words, results at MFW≥200 carry low statistical reliability; interpretation should center on MFW=30–50.
- The appearance of "approach toward Southey" differs qualitatively across split points (broad lean / selective single-text convergence / isolation). Results from multiple split points should not simply be summed to reinforce the hypothesis.
- Step 2 is completely unaffected by window/step size settings (it builds one vector per whole document). This differs from Step 1 and the GI method's windowImposters option — do not conflate the two.
